Brand guide · v1.0 · Bangladesh first, English and Bangla

The shop you open at 11, and the order that lands at 11:11.

Eleventory gives a small seller a real store, stock that adds up and customers who can pay the way they already pay. This guide holds the mark, colour, type, voice and the screens that carry them, so every page looks like one company wrote it.

The idea

A bubble, a lens and two rising bars.

The icon holds three ideas in one shape. A speech bubble for the conversations that sell on Facebook and WhatsApp. A magnifier for seeing what is really selling. Two bars that rise and read as eleven. It is a single colour, so it works on a shop sign, a parcel sticker and a phone screen.

Who we serve

A seller in Dhaka, Chattogram or Sylhet who runs a page on Facebook, ships with Pathao or Steadfast, takes bKash, Nagad or cash on delivery and keeps the stock count in a notebook.

What they fear

Selling something that is already gone. Chasing a courier for the cash. A store that looks like everyone else's. A tool that needs a developer.

What we promise

Stock that always matches the shelf. A shop that looks like theirs. Money that arrives where they expect it. Set up in an afternoon, in Bangla or English.

Colour

Rich teal, coral, a little sunflower

Teal does the work. Coral marks the one thing to act on or celebrate on a screen, such as the main button or a sale. Sunflower is the rare third voice, for a highlight or a badge. The canvas is a soft grey with a green cast. It is never pure white and never pure black.

Signature element

The rising bars

The two bars in the icon are the brand's unit. A row of them, climbing, is the loading indicator, the empty state, the divider before a section and the backdrop of the hero. The last bar is coral and means "this one just sold". Never more than one coral bar in a row.

Hero scroll story

The marketing page opens on a quiet row of bars. As you scroll, they climb, the last one turns coral, and the row becomes a store, then an order, then a delivered parcel.

Loading

The bars rise one after another, the last in coral. Under reduced motion it is a still, full row.

Charts and empty states

Sales charts use the same bar. A shop with no orders yet shows one flat coral bar waiting for company.

Voice

Plain, warm, specific

We talk the way a good shop assistant does: short sentences, real numbers, the seller's own words. We name things by what a seller recognises. A seller manages orders, not fulfilment workflows.

Say

Your first order is in. Packing slip is ready.

Only 3 left in stock. Reorder before Friday?

bKash payment received: ৳2,490.

Not

Unlock a seamless commerce experience.

Inventory threshold alert triggered.

Transaction successfully processed!

Rules for every line of copy

  • No em dashes. Use a full stop or a comma.
  • No "seamless", "unlock", "empower", "elevate", "revolutionise", "all-in-one".
  • No "not X, but Y" framing. Say the thing.
  • A button names its result: "Publish store", "Mark as shipped".
  • An error says what happened and the fix, without an apology.
  • Use real numbers and units: ৳, kg, "2 days", never "fast".

Write Bangla from scratch for a Bangla reader. Do not translate English word by word. Keep ৳ before the number, and keep brand and payment names (bKash, Nagad, Pathao) in Latin script.

Product presentation

A store that looks like the seller's

Each shop gets a tint, so a saree, a mug and a sneaker never share one grey box. The tile gets a soft colour from the product, one price, one flag. Sale flags are coral, new or limited flags are sunflower. These are drawn shapes standing in for seller photography.

Small sizes, same idea
